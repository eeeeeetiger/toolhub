You scanned a contract, exported a report, or saved a presentation — and the PDF is 40 MB. Email rejects it, Slack warns you, and your client is waiting. You need to shrink it. The question is: do you really want to upload that document to a random website?

## Why PDFs get so big

- **Scanned pages** are stored as images, not text.
- **Embedded photos** at full resolution.
- **Fonts and layers** that bloat the file.

## Compress a PDF without uploading it

Our [PDF compressor](/tools/pdf-compress) does the whole job in your browser:

1. Drop the PDF onto the page.
2. Choose a compression level (the tool re-encodes images inside the PDF).
3. Download the smaller file.

**Your PDF is never sent anywhere.** It's processed in your browser tab and the result downloads straight to your machine. For sensitive contracts, that privacy matters.

## What to expect

- Image-heavy PDFs shrink the most.
- Plain text PDFs are already small and won't shrink much — that's normal.
- If a single file is still too big after compressing, split it with our [PDF split tool](/tools/pdf-split) and send it in parts.

## Pair it with other local PDF tools

- [Merge PDFs](/tools/pdf-merge) to combine several documents first, then compress the result.
- [Extract pages](/tools/pdf-extract-pages) you don't need to cut weight before compressing.
- Need to turn paper into PDF? Our [image to PDF](/tools/image-to-pdf) tool keeps that step local too.

## Why local beats "free PDF sites"

Most free PDF compressors upload your file to their servers, process it, and email or link you the result. For a public brochure that's okay. For anything personal, financial, or confidential, processing it on your own device is the safer default — and just as fast.
