If you've ever plugged your iPhone into a Windows PC, you've probably seen files ending in `.heic` that simply won't open. That's Apple's HEIC (High Efficiency Image Container) format — great for saving space, terrible for sharing.

The usual advice is to upload your photos to a "free converter" website. But that means sending your personal photos (often family, travel, or work screenshots) to a stranger's server. There's a simpler, private way.

## Why HEIC is a problem

- **Windows and Android can't open it natively.** A `.heic` dropped into an email or a slide deck often shows up as a broken file.
- **Many tools and websites reject it.** Upload forms, CMS platforms, and printers frequently ask for JPG or PNG.
- **JPG is the universal format.** Converting to JPG makes the photo openable everywhere.

## Convert HEIC to JPG locally

With our [HEIC to JPG tool](/tools/heic-to-jpg), the conversion happens entirely inside your browser:

1. Drag your HEIC file onto the page (or click to choose it).
2. Wait a second — the image is decoded on your device using WebAssembly.
3. Download the JPG.

**Nothing is uploaded.** The file is read from your computer, converted in memory, and the result is handed back to you. Close the tab and the photo never touched a server.

## When to use JPG vs PNG

- **JPG** for photos — smaller files, perfect for sharing and email.
- **PNG** if you need a transparent background (photos usually don't).
