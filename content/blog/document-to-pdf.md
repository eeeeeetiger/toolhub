PDF is the format everyone accepts — but your source is often a Word doc, a slide deck, or a Markdown note. Converting shouldn't mean handing the document to a website, especially when it's a contract or a confidential draft.

## Word → PDF

Our [Word to PDF tool](/tools/word-to-pdf) reads the `.docx` in your browser and produces a PDF:

1. Drop the Word file in.
2. It's rendered and exported on your device.
3. Download the PDF.

## PowerPoint → PDF

For slide decks, [PowerPoint to PDF](/tools/ppt-to-pdf) turns each slide into a page — great for sharing a read-only version:

1. Add the `.pptx`.
2. Convert locally.
3. Download.

## Markdown → PDF

Writers and developers often work in Markdown. Our [Markdown to PDF tool](/tools/markdown-to-pdf) formats the text and exports a clean PDF, all in the browser.

## Why do it locally

Every one of these converters runs on your device, so **the document is never uploaded**. For anything with real content — quotes, contracts, drafts — that's the safe default.

## After converting

- The output PDF is often large with images; run [PDF compress](/tools/pdf-compress).
- Combine several converted docs with [PDF merge](/tools/pdf-merge).
- Add page numbers via [PDF page numbers](/tools/pdf-page-numbers).

## The bigger picture

A document workflow that stays on your machine — convert, merge, compress, watermark — avoids the quiet risk of cloud tools that keep copies of what you upload. Same result, none of the exposure.
