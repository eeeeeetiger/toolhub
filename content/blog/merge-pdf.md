You've got three PDFs — a cover page, a report, and an appendix — and you need to send them as one file. Merging should be simple, but most "merge PDF" sites ask you to upload every page first.

## When you need to merge PDFs

- Combining a signed page with the main contract.
- Assembling chapters of an ebook or thesis.
- Joining monthly statements into a yearly archive.

## Merge locally, keep it private

Our [PDF merge tool](/tools/pdf-merge) works in your browser:

1. Add the PDFs in the order you want.
2. Reorder them by dragging if needed.
3. Click merge and download the combined file.

Everything is processed **on your device**. The documents are never uploaded to a server, which is exactly what you want when the files contain personal or business information.

## After merging

- The combined file is often large — run it through our [PDF compressor](/tools/pdf-compress) before emailing.
- Need to pull a page back out later? Use [PDF extract pages](/tools/pdf-extract-pages).
- Want page numbers on the final doc? Our [PDF page numbers](/tools/pdf-page-numbers) tool adds them locally too.

## Build a private PDF workflow

A typical confidential flow stays entirely on your machine:

1. [Merge](/tools/pdf-merge) the pieces.
2. [Add page numbers](/tools/pdf-page-numbers) or a [watermark](/tools/pdf-watermark).
3. [Compress](/tools/pdf-compress) for delivery.

No step uploads a byte. Compare that to chained cloud tools where each one gets a copy of your document.
