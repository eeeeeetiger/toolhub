Sometimes you only want the sound: a lecture, a song in a clip, a voiceover, or a podcast recorded as video. Extracting the audio to MP3 lets you listen anywhere — and you shouldn't have to upload the video to do it.

## Common reasons to extract audio

- Make a ringtone from a video clip.
- Listen to a talk while commuting, without the video.
- Pull a soundtrack or sample for editing.

## Extract locally

Our [video to MP3 tool](/tools/video-to-mp3) runs ffmpeg in your browser:

1. Drop in the video (MP4, MOV, WebM, and more).
2. The audio track is extracted and encoded to MP3 on your device.
3. Download the MP3.

**The video is never uploaded.** It's read from your computer, processed in the tab, and the audio file is handed back. For anything personal, that's the safe way to do it.

## Before and after

- Trim the video first with [video cutter](/tools/video-cutter) if you only want a segment.
- Got an M4A recording instead of a video? Use [M4A to MP3](/tools/m4a-to-mp3).
- Need to clean up the audio? [Volume normalizer](/tools/volume-normalizer) or [silence trim](/tools/silence-trim) keep the whole job local.

## Why local extraction matters

Cloud "video to MP3" sites make a copy of your video on their server, process it, and send the audio back — meanwhile your file sits on someone else's disk. Doing it in the browser gives you the same MP3 with none of that exposure, and usually faster since there's no upload wait.
